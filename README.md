Cardiopulmonary diseases represent the leading causes of mortality in the United States, accounting for more than 800,000 deaths annually. Effective diagnosis, research, and scientific communication of these diseases require an accurate understanding of the complex spatial relationships among cardiac and thoracic structures. Due to the presence of adjacent organs and overlapping anatomical systems, the thoracic cavity represents a challenging environment for three-dimensional visualization. As volumetric medical imaging continues to advance, visual communication of complex anatomy is necessary for clear interpretation of scientific results, as well as quality assurance. However, existing visualization tools are primarily designed for general purpose rendering rather than standardized scientific representation. Default rendering parameters, such as arbitrary color assignments, opacity settings, and transfer functions can obscure anatomical relationships and reduce the consistency of three-dimensional visualizations. We present SPECTRA (Standardized Perceptual Encoding of Clinical Three-dimensional Representation of Anatomy), an open-source framework providing consistent visualization of segmented anatomical structures focusing on the thorax. SPECTRA produced two forms of animated three-dimensional visualization: orbital-view animations and animated exploded-view visualizations. Leveraging AI-assisted design, we developed perceptually coherent color lookup tables, distance-based opacity transfer functions for animated exploded-view visualizations and rendering parameters that improve the visualization of complex anatomical and spatial relationships. Using segmented thoracic anatomy as a representative application, SPECTRA establishes design principles emphasizing scientific consistency, perceptual separability, anatomical grouping through color harmony, and preservation of anatomical context. The proposed framework is evaluated against default visualization presets for automated thoracic segmentation to demonstrate improvements in visual consistency and structure separability. In addition to the visualization methodology, we release the complete open-source toolkit with lookup tables, rendering presets, and example animated visualizations to encourage standardized three-dimensional scientific communication in medical imaging. 

Keywords: anatomical segmentation, animated visualization, open-source framework, thoracic anatomy
